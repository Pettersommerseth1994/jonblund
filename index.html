Why it works anywhere

Thick walls beat most baby calls. They do not beat a phone call.

Ordinary baby calls talk over their own little radio link. Brick, concrete, old plaster and a floor in between all eat that signal, which is why so many of them crackle, cut out, or go silent in exactly the room you needed them in.

Before you start

What Jon Blund needs, and what it is not.

Better you read this here than find it out when you need it.

Two devices, and one has to be a phone

By the crib, anything with internet and a microphone will do. A spare iPad is lovely for it. The one you carry has to be a phone with coverage, because that is the one Jon Blund rings.

The listening device stays awake

Browsers stop the microphone when a screen locks, so that device keeps its screen on and stays plugged in. Jon Blund dims itself so the screen is not glaring beside the cot.

Save the number it calls from

Jon Blund sends you a contact card to pass on. Saved in the other phone, the call gets through even when everything else is silenced.

It is not a medical device

Jon Blund listens for crying. It does not track breathing, movement or anything else, and it never replaces someone checking on the baby.

Always test one call in a new place

A hotel room, a cabin, the grandparents’ spare room. Internet and coverage behave differently everywhere, so make Jon Blund ring you once before you trust it with a night.
